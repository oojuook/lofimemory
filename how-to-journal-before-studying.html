How to journal before studying.

The best pre-study journaling is short. You are not trying to write a full diary entry. You are trying to clear noise, choose one priority, and reduce the chance that stress or scrolling will steal the next block.

Start a short entryOpen Focus TreeStop-scrolling guide

A good pre-study journal takes two to five minutes

  1. Write the one task that matters most right now.
  2. Name the distraction most likely to interrupt you.
  3. Write what “done enough” looks like for this session.
  4. Choose the first step you will take when the timer starts.

What to write if you feel stressed

What feels loud?

Naming the loudest thought prevents it from quietly running the whole study block.

What can wait?

This question stops every worry from pretending it is urgent.

What is my first move?

Focus improves when the start of the session is obvious.

What will I do during my break?

A short plan for the break helps you avoid turning one pause into a long scroll.

A sample entry

“My main goal is to finish the biology notes for chapter three. I will probably want to check my phone after ten minutes. Done enough means I finish the summary page and the flashcards. My first step is to open the notes and highlight the key terms.”

Why it works

Pre-study journaling works because it gives your attention a clean landing point. Instead of entering the session with vague pressure, you enter it with a visible target and a calmer head.
