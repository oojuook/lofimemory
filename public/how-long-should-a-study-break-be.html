How long should a study break be?

A good study break is usually 5 to 15 minutes. It should be long enough to reset your attention but short enough that returning still feels easy.

Open calm games

The practical answer

5 minutes

Best after a short focus sprint or when you only need to stand, breathe, or write one thought.

10 minutes

Best for a relaxing browser game, a lofi reset, or a quick journal check-in.

15 minutes

Best after a longer study block when your brain feels tired but not fully drained.

What makes a break work

A study break should change your state without stealing your attention. That is why low-pressure games, stretching, water, music, and short journal entries often work better than opening a feed. Feeds are designed to continue. A good break has an ending.

How to avoid losing the session

  • Choose the break before you start studying.
  • Use one small activity, not five.
  • Set a return cue: one song, one puzzle, one entry, or one timer.
  • Write where to restart before you step away.

If your break makes returning feel harder, it was probably too stimulating. Pick a calmer activity next time.
