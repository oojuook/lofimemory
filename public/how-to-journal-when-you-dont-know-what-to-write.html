How to journal when you do not know what to write.

If you do not know what to write, make the page smaller. You do not need a perfect story. You only need one clear question, one honest sentence, or one short list that helps your thoughts settle into something usable.

The easiest starting prompts

Right now I feel...

This helps if the page feels blank because your feelings are still vague.

Today I keep thinking about...

This works well when one topic keeps returning and needs a place to land.

What I need most today is...

This turns journaling into a check-in instead of a performance.

One thing I want to remember is...

This is useful when you want the journal to feel practical and grounded.

Formats that reduce pressure

Three-line journal

Write what happened, how you feel, and what comes next.

Bullet-list journal

Lists count. They are especially helpful when your energy is low.

One-sentence journal

One true sentence is still a real entry and often easier to continue tomorrow.

Question-and-answer journal

Ask yourself one question and answer it honestly. That is enough.

What to avoid

Do not wait for the right mood, the perfect prompt, or a deep insight before you start. Journaling usually becomes easier after you begin, not before.
