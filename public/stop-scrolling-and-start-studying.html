The painkiller guide

Stop scrolling and start studying.

If you lose hours to infinite feeds when you should be studying, you don't need more "motivation." You need a lower-friction start. This guide shows how to use Lofi Memory as a quiet anchor to stop the scroll and stay with your work.

The pain: Why students get stuck in the scroll

Friction is high

Starting is the hardest part

Opening a heavy productivity app feels like more work. It's easier to stay on social media where the dopamine is immediate.

Attention is split

The "just one more" loop

Without a visual anchor, your brain looks for something to fill the silence. A quick check of your phone turns into a 40-minute scroll.

Noise is everywhere

Too many browser tabs

When your study space looks like your entertainment space, focus feels impossible. You need a dedicated "study room" tab.

The solution: Use Lofi Memory as your anchor

Visual Presence

The Focus Tree rule

The tree only grows while the page is visible. This tiny ritual creates a cost for switching tabs: you see exactly when you left your focus.

Immediate Calm

Lofi music by default

No searching for playlists. One click starts the radio, creating a familiar "study signal" that tells your brain it's time to work.

Low Friction

Journal as you finish

Don't track everything. Just write one sentence after your study block about what you did. It's easier to keep than a complex diary.

A 3-minute routine to break the scroll

Minute 1: Open Lofi Memory and start music.
Put your phone face down. Choose one wallpaper that feels quiet.
Minute 2: Write your one intention.
Type one small task in the Focus room intention field (e.g., "Read 5 pages" or "Draft the intro").
Minute 3: Begin with the Focus Tree visible.
Don't worry about being perfect. Just stay on the page and start. If you switch tabs, the tree stops—reminding you to come back.

Why this works for the student ICP

Lofi Memory is built for the "aesthetic study" and "cozy productivity" audience. It treats focus as a visual, relaxing ritual rather than a stressful deadline. By providing lofi music, a visible timer, and private journaling in one tab, it solves the problem of needing five different tools just to get started.

Stop-scroll check-ins

Are you scrolling right now? Close this guide, open the Focus Tree, and try a 10-minute block. Rebuilding focus happens in small, visible wins, not big life changes.
