What should I write in my journal?

Write what is taking up space in your mind. A journal does not need to sound beautiful. It only needs to be honest enough to help you notice what you feel, what happened, and what you want to do next.

Start with one line

Start with these five categories

1. What happened: A simple diary-style record of your day, class, study session, or conversation.
2. What you felt: The mood underneath the event, even if it feels messy or unfinished.
3. What you learned: A small insight about your habits, stress, focus, or relationships.
4. What you need: Rest, clarity, a plan, food, movement, support, or a break.
5. What comes next: One gentle next action that would make life 1% easier.

If you feel blank

Blankness is also something to write about. Try: “I do not know what to write because…” or “The thing I am avoiding naming is…” The first sentence does not need to be good. It only needs to open the door.

For students

Students can use a journal to close a study loop. Write what you studied, what clicked, what still feels confusing, and where to restart tomorrow. This turns your journal into a focus tool, not just a feelings page.

For overthinking

Write the thought exactly as it appears. Then ask: what is fact, what is assumption, and what else could be true? This turns a mental loop into something you can examine.
